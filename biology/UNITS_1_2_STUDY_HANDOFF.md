## Study-design scope

Use the VCE Biology Study Design 2022–2026 as the current known framework for this project, and verify the applicable design with the student's school before relying on it for a later assessment year. Do not claim that every textbook subtopic is a separate prescribed dot point.

### Unit 1 — How do organisms regulate their functions?

**AOS1: How do cells function?**
- Cell structure, prokaryotic/eukaryotic cells, plant/animal cells, organelles and compartmentalisation.
- Surface-area-to-volume ratio and limits on cell size.
- Plasma membrane structure, selective permeability and transport: diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion and active transport.
- Cell cycle, mitosis, cytokinesis, cell-cycle regulation, apoptosis, cancer, stem cells and differentiation.

**AOS2: How do plant and animal systems function?**
- Plant cell specialisation and organisation into tissues for water intake, movement and loss.
- Xylem, phloem, roots, leaves, stomata, guard cells and transpiration.
- Animal cell specialisation and organisation into tissues, organs and systems.
- Digestive, endocrine and excretory systems.
- Homeostasis: stimulus-response models, feedback loops, thermoregulation, blood glucose and water balance.
- Malfunctions: type 1 diabetes, hypoglycaemia and hyperthyroidism.

**AOS3: How is scientific inquiry used to investigate cellular and body systems?**
- Research questions, aims, hypotheses, predictions, variables, controls, methodology and sampling.
- Data collection, organisation, tables, graphs, patterns and relationships.
- Accuracy, precision, repeatability, reproducibility, validity, errors, uncertainty and outliers.
- Evaluate methods and limitations; draw evidence-based conclusions; communicate scientifically.

### Unit 2 — How does inheritance impact on diversity?

**AOS1: How is inheritance explained?**
- DNA, genes, alleles, genomes, loci, homologous chromosomes, autosomes and sex chromosomes.
- Diploid/haploid cells, chromosome number and karyotypes.
- Meiosis, crossing over, independent assortment and fertilisation.
- Genotype, phenotype, homozygous/heterozygous, dominant/recessive, codominance and incomplete dominance.
- Genetic, environmental and epigenetic influences.
- Monohybrid crosses, test crosses, pedigrees, autosomal and sex-linked inheritance, linked genes and independent assortment.

**AOS2: How do inherited adaptations impact on diversity?**
- Asexual and sexual reproduction; benefits and limitations.
- Reproductive cloning, its process, applications and implications.
- Genetic diversity and its importance to populations.
- Structural, physiological and behavioural adaptations.
- Species interdependence, predator-prey relationships, keystone species and ecosystem effects.
- Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander ecological knowledge and contributions to ecosystem understanding and management.

**AOS3: How is biological knowledge applied to bioethical issues?**
- Secondary research; distinguish primary/secondary data, evidence, opinion and anecdote.
- Assess source quality, validity, authority, reliability and bias.
- Organise, analyse and synthesise secondary information.
- Evaluate models, representations, assumptions and limitations.
- Consider social, economic, legal and political contexts.
- Identify and analyse bioethical issues and stakeholders.
- Apply consequences-based, duty/rule-based and virtue-based ethical approaches.
- Use ethical concepts including integrity, justice, beneficence, non-maleficence and respect.
- Develop and communicate a justified position with credible evidence and acknowledged limitations.

## Priority: build the Unit 2 AOS3 deck next

Create additional cards in a dedicated AOS3 module. Use scenario-based prompts and answer structures. Prioritise:

1. **Ethical approaches:** distinguish and apply consequences-based, duty/rule-based and virtue-based reasoning to the same scenario.
2. **Ethical concepts:** justice, respect, beneficence, non-maleficence and integrity; identify conflicts between them.
3. **Stakeholders:** identify affected individuals, families, researchers, communities, governments, industries and ecosystems; explain differing interests.
4. **Evidence evaluation:** primary vs secondary sources; evidence vs opinion; authority, validity, reliability, bias, limitations and conflicting findings.
5. **Issue analysis:** identify the biological science, potential benefits, risks, uncertainty, distribution of benefits/harms and wider social/economic/legal/political factors.
6. **Justified conclusion:** make a clear judgement, support it with evidence, apply ethical reasoning, acknowledge counterarguments/limitations and explain why the position is strongest.
7. **Unfamiliar scenarios:** practise transferring the framework to cases the student has not memorised.

Potential practice contexts (use as examples, not as a requirement to memorise every topic): genetic testing, embryo selection, reproductive technologies, cloning, genetic modification in agriculture, conservation and introduced species, genetic diversity, biomimicry, and use/protection of Indigenous ecological knowledge.

## Card-design rules

- Prefer integrated, high-yield cards over many tiny cards.
- Use one clear task per card; avoid overloaded backs.
- Include application prompts such as “apply all three ethical approaches to this case”.
- Answers should be concise but contain the reasoning needed for full marks.
- Include common misconceptions and contrasts where they are useful.
- Scenario cards must provide enough context to answer without assuming facts not supplied.
- Keep VCAA terminology accurate.
- Do not invent claims, quotations, statistics or scientific evidence for case studies.
- Clearly distinguish study-design requirements from optional illustrative examples.
- Add a unique ID and topic tag to each card when exporting structured data.
